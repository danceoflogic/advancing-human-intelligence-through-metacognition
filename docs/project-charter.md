# Project charter

## Mission

Investigate whether people can improve measurable, real-world cognitive effectiveness by learning to recognise, select, monitor, and revise reasoning strategies, and assess whether these improvements transfer to unfamiliar tasks.

## Working definitions

- **Metacognition:** monitoring and regulation of one's own cognitive processes, including planning and evaluating.
- **Reasoning strategy:** a repeatable approach to a task, such as decomposition, checking assumptions, or comparing hypotheses.
- **Strategy selection:** adapting one's method to the demands of the task.
- **Effective cognitive performance:** objectively measured accuracy, decision quality, calibration, or time-adjusted task performance.
- **Near transfer:** gains on new but closely related tasks.
- **Far transfer:** gains on tasks substantially different from those practised; define the distinction operationally for each study.
- **General intelligence:** a distinct construct; not inferred from improvement on a few exercises.

## In scope

Literature review, conceptual models, strategy libraries, educational exercises, exploratory pilot designs, and transparent measurement.

## Out of scope at launch

Claims of increasing IQ; diagnosis or treatment; unsupervised experiments involving children; claims of a validated intervention; or publishing private participant records.

## Initial deliverables

1. Evidence map including supporting and conflicting findings.
2. Versioned strategy taxonomy and proposed selection rules.
3. Clearly specified and appropriately reviewed experimental protocols.
4. Reproducible reporting of positive, negative, and inconclusive outcomes.

## Success criteria

Clarity of definitions, feasible and reliable measurement, documented methods, and careful reporting. A positive experimental effect is **not** required for this project to be useful.

## Provenance

Concept originated in a conversation on 2026-10-11 concerning childhood learning of thinking patterns and the possibility of improving adult reasoning through metacognition. These are research proposals, not established findings from this project.
