# Draft N-of-1 feasibility pilot

**Design sketch only:** not preregistered, not conducted, and not grounds for claims of causation.

## Aim

Assess the feasibility of following a small metacognitive toolkit and measuring performance on genuinely unseen problems. A future confirmatory study would require stronger experimental controls and independent evaluation.

## Proposed intervention

1. Define the task and a measurable goal.
2. List crucial assumptions and uncertainties.
3. Choose a task-appropriate reasoning strategy.
4. Estimate confidence where meaningful.
5. Verify results and search for errors.
6. Record feedback for future strategy selection.

The intervention's exact content and duration must be fixed before testing.

## Proposed procedure

1. Develop versioned task pools with comparable difficulty across at least two domains.
2. Establish baseline performance using new items; measure time and confidence when applicable.
3. Predefine intervention, active comparison, task allocation and timing. Consider randomised and counterbalanced blocks for a stronger design.
4. Record adherence and outcomes using timestamped, privacy-safe logs.
5. Reserve unseen items for near-transfer and far-transfer testing.
6. Add a delayed follow-up for retention.
7. Report missing trials, deviations, exploratory analyses, and null findings.

## Candidate outcomes

**Primary, to be selected in advance:** accuracy against an objective or independently scored rubric on held-out tasks.

**Secondary:** duration, detected-error types, and confidence calibration (for suitable probabilistic judgements).

**Process:** accurate selection and use of the designated strategy.

## Threats to validity

Practice effects, fatigue, familiarity, expectations, unequal time-on-task, feedback leakage, regression to the mean, narrow tasks and unblinded scoring. One participant cannot establish a population-level effect.

## Safety and privacy

Do not manipulate sleep, medications, or health. Activities should be low-risk and voluntary. Any recruitment requires appropriate ethics and privacy review. No identifiable individual data should be published.
